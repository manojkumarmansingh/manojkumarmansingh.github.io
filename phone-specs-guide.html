How to read phone specifications without being fooled by the numbers

Phone specifications are written to sound impressive, not to tell you how the phone will feel. Here is what each number actually means.

Camera megapixels

More megapixels means more detail in bright light and larger files. It does not mean better photos in the evening, where the size of the sensor and how the software processes the image matter far more. A well tuned 12 megapixel camera regularly beats a badly tuned 108 megapixel one. Ignore the number and look at sample photos taken indoors.

Refresh rate

90 Hz or 120 Hz makes scrolling and animations look smoother. It is one of the few specifications you can feel immediately, and it is worth having. The trade off is battery, though most phones drop to a lower rate automatically when the screen is static.

RAM

6 GB is comfortable for most people. 8 GB helps if you keep many apps open or play heavy games. 12 GB and above is mostly marketing on a phone, because Android manages memory aggressively anyway. Storage matters more than RAM for most buyers, and 128 GB fills faster than people expect.

Battery capacity and charging wattage

Capacity is in mAh, and 5,000 mAh is now standard. Charging wattage tells you how fast it refills, but only if the charger in the box supports it. Check what is included, because several brands no longer ship a charger.

Processor names

"Octa core" tells you nothing. Core count has been eight on almost everything for years. What matters is the specific chip model and how old it is. A two year old flagship chip usually beats a brand new budget chip, and a phone with a good chip and 6 GB of RAM will feel better for longer than one with a weak chip and 12 GB.

Words to discount

"AI camera", "AI engine", "smart performance" and similar phrases have no standard meaning. They are not lies exactly, but they are not specifications either. Look for the numbers you can compare, and read reviews that mention real use.

The specification that never appears

Software support. How many years of Android updates and security patches the phone gets matters more than any single number on the box, because a phone without security updates becomes a liability. Check the brand's published policy before you buy.

The short version: feel the screen, check the chip, take storage seriously, and find out how many years of updates you are buying.

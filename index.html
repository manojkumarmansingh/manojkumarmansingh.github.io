Why your Android phone slows down after a year, and the 6 fixes that actually work

By Manoj Kumar Mansingh · Updated 20 November 2026 · 8 min read

Almost every Android phone feels fast in its first month and tired by its twelfth. It is rarely because the phone has "worn out" in some vague sense. In most cases the cause is one of four things: storage nearly full, too many apps waking up in the background, an ageing battery that cannot deliver peak power, or a cache and app set that has grown unchecked.

The fixes below are ordered by how much difference they make per minute of effort. Do them in order and stop when the phone feels acceptable. There is no need to do all six.

In this guide

  1. Get storage below 80% full
  2. Cut background app activity
  3. Clear the cache properly
  4. Turn off animations
  5. Check battery health
  6. Know when to stop fixing

1. Get storage below 80% full

This is the single biggest cause and the easiest to check. Flash storage needs free space to write and shuffle data. When a phone is above roughly 85–90% full, the system starts spending time finding room instead of doing work, and the whole interface feels sticky.

Open Settings → Storage and look at the number, not the coloured bar. If you are above 80%, the fastest wins are usually:

  • Photos and videos: back them up, then delete the local copies.
  • WhatsApp media: the single largest hidden consumer on most Indian phones. Clear the forwarded videos and images you will never open again.
  • Downloads folder: old PDFs, APK files and screenshots.
  • Apps you have not opened in three months: uninstall, not just disable.

Getting from 92% to 70% full often does more for perceived speed than any "booster" app ever will.

2. Cut background app activity

Every app you install can ask to run in the background. Twenty apps doing that quietly will keep the processor busy and the memory full, which is why the phone hesitates when you switch apps.

Go to Settings → Apps → [app] → Battery → Restricted for anything you do not need notifications from in real time: shopping apps, games, news apps, most utilities. Keep messaging, banking and anything with OTP or delivery alerts unrestricted.

Do not use "RAM booster" or "cleaner" apps. Android manages memory itself. These apps kill processes that the system then has to restart, which costs more battery and makes the phone slower, not faster.

3. Clear the cache properly

Clearing cache is safe; clearing "data" is not, because that wipes your logins and settings. For a stubborn app, use Settings → Apps → [app] → Storage → Clear cache. For the whole system, a reboot once a week does most of the same work.

If your phone has been updated across several major Android versions, a factory reset, after a full backup, is the honest fix. It is a two-hour job and it genuinely restores most of the original speed, because it removes years of accumulated app cruft.

4. Turn off animations

This does not make the phone faster, but it makes it feel faster, which is what most people are actually complaining about. Enable Developer options (tap Build number seven times in About phone), then set Window animation scale, Transition animation scale and Animator duration scale to 0.5x. Many people prefer 0.5x over off, since it keeps the interface from feeling abrupt.

5. Check the battery

A battery that has lost a lot of capacity cannot always supply peak current, so the phone limits processor speed to avoid shutting down. That shows up as lag when the battery is below 20% or in the heat.

Most Android phones show battery status under Settings → Battery → Battery health or in the manufacturer's own service app; if yours does not, a service centre can read it in a minute. Below about 80% of original capacity on a phone that is two years old, a battery replacement is usually cheaper and more effective than a new phone.

6. Know when to stop fixing

If you have done the first three steps and the phone is still slow, the limiting factor is usually the hardware itself: 3–4 GB of RAM with a modern app set, or a processor from several generations ago. No setting fixes that. At that point the sensible options are a battery replacement if the phone is otherwise fine, or a new phone. The honest test is whether the lag happens in the first five minutes after a reboot. If it does, it is the hardware.

The short version: get storage under 80%, restrict background battery use for shopping and game apps, reboot weekly, and replace the battery at the two-year mark. That covers most cases.

Menu names vary slightly between Samsung, Xiaomi, Realme, Vivo and stock Android. If a path does not match your phone, search the setting name in your phone's own Settings search box. Every modern Android build has one.

The Android permission that lets a scam app watch your UPI PIN

By Manoj Kumar Mansingh · Updated 20 November 2026 · 6 min read

UPI fraud used to mean a phone call from someone pretending to be from your bank, talking you into reading out an OTP. That still happens. But a newer kind of fraud does not need you to trust anyone on the phone, and it does not need your PIN either. It needs one permission on your phone that most people grant without reading it.

On 17 November 2026, Livemint reported that NPCI, the body that runs UPI, has started talking to Google about exactly this problem. The fraudsters are not tricking users into sharing details. They are installing apps that misuse Android permissions to interfere with other apps on the phone, including the payment app you are about to type your PIN into.

The permission in question

It is called Display over other apps. On some phones it appears as Appear on top or Draw over other apps. In plain language, it lets one app put its own window on top of whatever you are looking at.

That is useful for a genuine app. A call bubble, a screen recorder, a floating timer all need it. The problem is what it allows when the app is not genuine. A malicious app can draw a convincing copy of a payment screen over the real one. You type your UPI PIN into what looks like your payment app, and the layer on top is the thing receiving it.

The uncomfortable part: nothing looks wrong while it happens. The screen looks normal, the app looks normal, and the payment may even go through. The theft is the copy of your PIN, used later.

Check this setting today, it takes two minutes

The menu name varies by brand, but it is in the same place on almost every Android phone:

  • Stock Android and Pixel: Settings → Apps → Special app access → Display over other apps
  • Samsung: Settings → Apps → the three dot menu → Special access → Appear on top
  • Xiaomi, Redmi, Poco: Settings → Apps → Manage apps → Special permissions → Display pop-up windows while running in the background
  • Vivo, Oppo, Realme: Settings → Apps → Special app access, or search "appear on top" in the Settings search box

You will get a list of every app that is allowed to draw on top of others. Read it slowly. Turn off anything you do not recognise, and turn off anything that has no honest reason to be there. A shopping app, a photo editor or a game does not need to draw over your banking app. A screen recorder might. Be strict.

The second permission to look at: Accessibility

While you are in there, check Settings → Accessibility → Downloaded apps. Accessibility access was built for screen readers and switch devices. It also lets an app read what is on your screen and tap things on your behalf, which is why banking trojans want it badly.

The rule is simple. If an app you installed in the last few months has accessibility access and you did not turn it on for a disability or a genuinely useful feature, turn it off now.

Stop side-loaded apps at the door

Almost every one of these cases starts with an app installed from outside the Play Store, usually an APK file sent over WhatsApp or offered on a website. Two settings make that much harder to do by accident:

  • Go to Settings → Apps → Special app access → Install unknown apps. Turn it off for your browser and for messaging apps. Then a link cannot quietly install something.
  • Before installing anything from the Play Store, check the developer name, the number of downloads and the recent reviews. A banking or government app with 500 installs and five reviews is not the real app.

If money has already moved

Call your bank's fraud line immediately and ask them to freeze the account and flag the transaction. Then report it at cybercrime.gov.in or on the national cybercrime helpline 1930. Speed matters more than anything else here: the earlier a fraudulent transfer is reported, the better the chance the receiving account is still frozen with the money in it.

The short version

Open your phone's settings, find Display over other apps, and remove every app you cannot justify. Then look at Accessibility and Install unknown apps. Those three screens are where this kind of fraud lives, and clearing them out costs less time than a single failed transaction.

Background: Livemint report of 17 November 2026 on NPCI's discussions with Google. Menu names differ between brands and software versions, so if a path does not match your phone, search the permission name in your phone's own Settings search box.

Your Android version does not keep you safe. This number does.

By Manoj Kumar Mansingh · Updated 20 November 2026 · 5 min read

On 4 November 2026, India's computer emergency response team, CERT-In, issued a high severity advisory about security holes inside Android itself. It covers phones running Android 14, 15, 16, 16 QPR2 and Android 17, which is most of the Android phones in the country.

The uncomfortable detail is in the wording. Some of these flaws sit in the Framework and System components, and CERT-In warned that an attacker could use them to run malicious code remotely, in some cases without you tapping a bad link or installing a fake app. That is a different class of risk from the scam messages most of us have learned to spot.

What actually matters: the patch level

Here is the part almost nobody checks. Google fixes these flaws in its monthly security bulletin, and the fix reaches your phone as a small update. Your phone records exactly which fix it has, and you can read it in ten seconds.

Open Settings, then About phone, then Android version. You are looking for a line that says Android security update or Security patch level. For this advisory, the safe values are 1 November 2026 or 5 November 2026, or anything later.

If that date is months old, your phone is missing fixes for problems that are already public. That is the real measure of whether you are protected, and it is why a phone on the newest Android version with a stale patch can be less safe than an older phone that updates regularly.

Quick test: if the security patch date is older than about two months, update the phone today. Anything before September 2026 for this advisory is worth fixing immediately.

How to update, and what to do when it says "up to date"

  • Go to Settings and search for System update or Software update, then tap Check for update.
  • Updates roll out in batches by region and model, so "no update available" often just means your batch has not arrived. Check again in a few days.
  • If it has been weeks, clear the cache of Google Play services in Settings, Apps, and try again. That clears the update check on many phones.
  • For phones that no longer receive updates at all, the honest answer is that the manufacturer has ended support. Check the brand's support page for your model. If it stopped years ago, no setting will protect you, and that is a reason to consider replacing the phone rather than installing another cleaner app.

Three habits that matter more than the version number

Update the moment it is offered. The gap between a fix being published and phones installing it is exactly the window attackers work in, because the details become public when Google publishes the bulletin.

Keep Play Protect on. It is in the Play Store under your profile, then Play Protect. It scans installed apps and warns about known harmful ones. Turning it off to install something is almost always a bad trade.

Treat "no update for my model" as information, not a failure. Budget phones often get two years of security updates and then stop. If yours has stopped, keep it off banking apps and use it for what it still does well.

The short version

Open Settings, find your security patch level, and check the date. If it is November 2026 or later, you have the fixes from this advisory and you can stop reading. If it is older, update today, and if your phone no longer receives updates at all, know that no app from the Play Store will fix that.

Background: CERT-In vulnerability note CIVN-2026-0536, published 4 November 2026, and Google's Android Security Bulletin for November 2026. Menu names differ between brands, so search the setting name in your phone's own Settings search box if a path does not match.

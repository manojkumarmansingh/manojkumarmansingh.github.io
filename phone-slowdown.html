Why your Android phone slows down after a year, and the 6 fixes that actually work

By Manoj Kumar Mansingh · Updated 20 November 2026 · 8 min read

Almost every Android phone feels fast in its first month and tired by its twelfth. It is rarely because the phone has "worn out" in some vague sense. In most cases the cause is one of four things: storage nearly full, too many apps waking up in the background, an ageing battery that cannot deliver peak power, or a cache and app set that has grown unchecked.

The fixes below are ordered by how much difference they make per minute of effort. Do them in order and stop when the phone feels acceptable. There is no need to do all six.

In this guide

  1. Get storage below 80% full
  2. Cut background app activity
  3. Clear the cache properly
  4. Turn off animations
  5. Check battery health
  6. Know when to stop fixing

1. Get storage below 80% full

This is the single biggest cause and the easiest to check. Flash storage needs free space to write and shuffle data. When a phone is above roughly 85–90% full, the system starts spending time finding room instead of doing work, and the whole interface feels sticky.

Open Settings → Storage and look at the number, not the coloured bar. If you are above 80%, the fastest wins are usually:

  • Photos and videos: back them up, then delete the local copies.
  • WhatsApp media: the single largest hidden consumer on most Indian phones. Clear the forwarded videos and images you will never open again.
  • Downloads folder: old PDFs, APK files and screenshots.
  • Apps you have not opened in three months: uninstall, not just disable.

Getting from 92% to 70% full often does more for perceived speed than any "booster" app ever will.

2. Cut background app activity

Every app you install can ask to run in the background. Twenty apps doing that quietly will keep the processor busy and the memory full, which is why the phone hesitates when you switch apps.

Go to Settings → Apps → [app] → Battery → Restricted for anything you do not need notifications from in real time: shopping apps, games, news apps, most utilities. Keep messaging, banking and anything with OTP or delivery alerts unrestricted.

Do not use "RAM booster" or "cleaner" apps. Android manages memory itself. These apps kill processes that the system then has to restart, which costs more battery and makes the phone slower, not faster.

3. Clear the cache properly

Clearing cache is safe; clearing "data" is not, because that wipes your logins and settings. For a stubborn app, use Settings → Apps → [app] → Storage → Clear cache. For the whole system, a reboot once a week does most of the same work.

If your phone has been updated across several major Android versions, a factory reset, after a full backup, is the honest fix. It is a two-hour job and it genuinely restores most of the original speed, because it removes years of accumulated app cruft.

4. Turn off animations

This does not make the phone faster, but it makes it feel faster, which is what most people are actually complaining about. Enable Developer options (tap Build number seven times in About phone), then set Window animation scale, Transition animation scale and Animator duration scale to 0.5x. Many people prefer 0.5x over off, since it keeps the interface from feeling abrupt.

5. Check the battery

A battery that has lost a lot of capacity cannot always supply peak current, so the phone limits processor speed to avoid shutting down. That shows up as lag when the battery is below 20% or in the heat.

Most Android phones show battery status under Settings → Battery → Battery health or in the manufacturer's own service app; if yours does not, a service centre can read it in a minute. Below about 80% of original capacity on a phone that is two years old, a battery replacement is usually cheaper and more effective than a new phone.

6. Know when to stop fixing

If you have done the first three steps and the phone is still slow, the limiting factor is usually the hardware itself: 3–4 GB of RAM with a modern app set, or a processor from several generations ago. No setting fixes that. At that point the sensible options are a battery replacement if the phone is otherwise fine, or a new phone. The honest test is whether the lag happens in the first five minutes after a reboot. If it does, it is the hardware.

The short version: get storage under 80%, restrict background battery use for shopping and game apps, reboot weekly, and replace the battery at the two-year mark. That covers most cases.

Menu names vary slightly between Samsung, Xiaomi, Realme, Vivo and stock Android. If a path does not match your phone, search the setting name in your phone's own Settings search box. Every modern Android build has one.

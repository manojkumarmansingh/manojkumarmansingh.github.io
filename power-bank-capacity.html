Why a 20,000 mAh power bank does not charge your phone four times

A 20,000 mAh power bank and a 5,000 mAh phone battery look like a simple sum: four charges. In reality you will get two and a half to three. Nothing is broken, and the explanation is worth knowing before you buy.

The voltage difference

Power bank capacity is quoted at the internal battery voltage, about 3.7 volts. Your phone charges at 5 volts or more over USB. Converting up costs energy, so the usable capacity at 5 volts is lower than the printed number. This alone accounts for a large part of the gap.

Conversion and heat losses

Every conversion step loses some energy as heat. A typical power bank delivers somewhere between 60 and 75 percent of its rated capacity to the phone. That is normal, not a defect, and cheap power banks sit at the bottom of that range.

The phone's own losses

The phone also loses energy while charging, and if you use it during charging, some of the power goes to the screen and processor instead of the battery.

What the honest numbers look like

A 10,000 mAh power bank realistically gives a 5,000 mAh phone about one and a bit full charges. A 20,000 mAh unit gives roughly two and a half to three. If you need three full charges reliably, buy 20,000 mAh or above from a brand that states its rated capacity at 5 volts, which some now do.

What to check before buying

  • Output wattage. A power bank that outputs 10 watts will charge a modern phone slowly. Look for 20 watts or more if your phone supports fast charging.
  • Rated capacity at 5 volts. If the box states it, that is the honest number. If it only states the 3.7 volt figure, assume 65 percent.
  • Airline limits. Cabin rules are usually expressed in watt hours, and 100 Wh is the common ceiling. A 20,000 mAh bank is about 74 Wh, so it is allowed in hand baggage, not in checked luggage.
  • Brand and certification. Power banks contain lithium cells. Buy from a known brand with a BIS mark rather than the cheapest listing.

The short version: assume about two thirds of the printed capacity actually reaches your phone, and check the output wattage before anything else.

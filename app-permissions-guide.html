What each Android app permission actually lets an app do

Permission prompts arrive at the worst moment, usually while you are trying to do something else, so most people tap Allow. Here is what each one actually grants, in plain language.

The ordinary ones

Camera. Take photos and record video while the app is open. A scanner app needs it. A flashlight app does not.

Microphone. Record audio. Voice notes and calls need it. A photo editor does not.

Contacts. Read your address book. Messaging apps use it to find friends. Most others have no reason to.

Photos and media. Read files you select. Modern Android lets you grant access to chosen photos only, which is the setting to prefer.

Notifications. Send you alerts. Harmless, but a good thing to prune when an app gets noisy.

The three that deserve real caution

Accessibility. Built for screen readers and switch devices, it also allows an app to read everything on your screen and tap on your behalf. A banking trojan's favourite permission. Only grant it to apps you deliberately chose for accessibility.

Display over other apps. Lets an app draw on top of others. Useful for call bubbles and screen recorders, dangerous when a fake window sits over a payment screen.

Install unknown apps. Lets a browser or messaging app install software from outside the Play Store. Leave this off unless you are deliberately installing something.

SMS and call permissions

Reading SMS was once common for OTP autofill. Google now restricts it heavily, and a legitimate app rarely needs it. If a non-messaging app asks to read your messages, that is a warning sign, not a feature.

How to review what you have already granted

Open Settings, then Privacy, then Permission manager. It lists every permission and every app that holds it, which is far easier to audit than going app by app. Ten minutes there is worth more than any security app you could install.

The short version: grant camera and microphone only to apps that obviously need them, treat accessibility and display-over-other-apps as high risk, and keep install-unknown-apps off.

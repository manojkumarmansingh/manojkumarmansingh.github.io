AI agents stopped being a demo this month. Here is what is worth trying

By Manoj Kumar Mansingh · Updated 26 November 2026 · 6 min read

For about two years, "AI agent" meant a video of something booking a flight while a developer watched. In the last two weeks of November 2026, that changed quietly. A dozen agent products shipped in a single week, several of them priced and open to anyone, and a few of them run somewhere you already are: WhatsApp, Telegram, Slack, or a terminal.

I have not tested most of these. What follows is what each one claims, what it costs, and which ones are worth your time if you are not running a company. Treat vendor claims as claims.

What actually launched this month

Three dates matter. On 19 November, Hercules launched Agents, which you describe in plain language and which then runs on its own, on a schedule or a trigger. It connects to Slack, Microsoft Teams, Google Chat, Linear, GitHub, Telegram, WhatsApp and X, and its selling point is that it rewrites its own instructions after reading back through its past runs. Plans start at free with 15 AI credits a month, then 25 dollars and 199 dollars a month.

On 20 November, Cognition shipped Devin Bots, persistent engineering agents that keep their own memory and own an ongoing goal, such as keeping a repository alive, and report back only the decisions that need a human. It is aimed at engineering teams, not individuals.

On 25 November, two consumer-facing ones arrived. MyClaw 3.0 lets you pick an agent engine, give it a role, and run it on a cloud computer that keeps working after you close your laptop, with agents sharing one memory and work assigned from the web, Slack, Telegram or WhatsApp. Airbot calls itself an AI chief of staff: it watches your CRM, email, calendar and Slack, then messages you first on WhatsApp or iMessage with a priority and a drafted next step for you to approve.

The pattern worth noticing: the interface is no longer a chat window on a website. It is WhatsApp, Telegram or Slack. That is why these are suddenly relevant to people who are not developers.

What is not for you yet

A large share of this month's launches are enterprise products with no public price: Salesforce Bridget for connecting browser-based business apps, Chargebee Arc for finance workflows, Clay's global agent for sales teams, Swan's named workers, and Amazon Connect Resolutions for refunds and claims. If you see a "contact sales" button, the product is not aimed at an individual and you should not spend an evening chasing it.

For developers there is one genuinely useful piece of plumbing: Google's Gemini Tool Retrieval, in preview from 19 November, which lets an agent with hundreds of tools load only the ones it actually needs. That is a token cost fix, not something a normal user will ever see.

Before you connect one to your accounts

An agent is different from a chatbot. A chatbot answers. An agent acts, and to act it needs access. Three things to check before you hand one over:

  • What it can read. If it connects to your Gmail or calendar, it can read the lot unless the product offers scoped access. Look for a permissions screen, not a marketing page.
  • What it can do without asking. Hercules, for example, runs on triggers with nobody watching. Know which actions are automatic and which need your approval, and prefer tools that show a proposal before committing.
  • What it costs per run. Several of these charge real model cost per action, on top of the subscription. A free tier of 15 credits a month disappears in an afternoon of experimenting.

If you want to try one this week

Start with something that fails safely. Give an agent a task with no money and no personal data in it: summarising news you already read, drafting replies you will send yourself, or keeping a list up to date. Watch what it does for a week before you let it touch your inbox.

The free tiers are the right place to begin. Hercules at zero cost, Omi CLI with monthly free credits if you write code, and MyClaw if you want to see what an always-on agent feels like from Telegram or WhatsApp.

The short version

Agents became a product category this month rather than a promise. The useful ones for an individual run in a messaging app you already use and cost little or nothing to try. The rest are enterprise tools with sales calls attached. Connect one to a task that cannot hurt you, and read the permissions screen before you connect it to anything that can.

Sources: vendor launch posts and changelogs for Hercules Agents, Devin Bots, MyClaw 3.0, Airbot, Omi CLI, Gemini Tool Retrieval, Salesforce Bridget, Chargebee Arc, Clay, Swan and Amazon Connect Resolutions, dated 17 to 25 November 2026. Prices are in US dollars before tax and were correct on the dates published. None of these products were tested for this article.
